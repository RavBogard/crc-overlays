# CRC Overlays — UI/UX review and recommendations

Reviewed 2026-09-14 against the live console at crc-overlays.vercel.app (signed in as Daniel, Administrator). Every page was walked at laptop and wide widths; the editor, publish flow, Live control search/preview, and Account were exercised. Four independent audits (information architecture, operator task flows, visual/copy, bloat inventory) were run over the captures and reconciled here. Source material and the four audit reports are attached alongside this file.

## The diagnosis in one paragraph

The app is not missing anything and nothing in it is badly built. It is saying everything two or three times, giving every noun its own card and its own page, and presenting the editor's world and the operator's world with equal weight to everyone. Concretely: the header stack plus the disconnect banner and status strip consume roughly half of a 900-pixel screen on Live control before the first graphic is reachable; the phrase "this does not change what is live" appears about nineteen times across the product (seven on the editor screen alone); nine top-level destinations exist where three of them (Service log, Source review, Health) are things an operator should never see and two more (Setup, Help) are read once; and the same vocabulary is used for different things ("Library" is a nav tab and the right-hand column of Live control; "Panel" is a layout and a slide; "Published" is a lifecycle state, a count, and a header pill that coexists with a toast saying "Nothing has been published yet"). That is what "bloated and hard to find things" feels like from the inside.

## Two actual bugs found along the way

First, a hard load of `/author` (typed URL, refresh, or an invitation link) while signed in as Administrator shows the "Open the library — Sign in / or use the existing admin key / Control key" gate. Navigating to Library from inside the app works. Reproduced twice. Consequences: any new invitee's first impression is a locked door, and the legacy Control-key path is advertised to every ordinary user. This is a session-check bug on that route and it should be fixed before any redesign.

Second, on Source review the page's own "Library" link and "Check sources" button render on top of the nav pills, overlapping "Account". A page action bar is being laid out in the header's grid.

## The proposed shape

Four destinations, filtered by role, in task order: **Live · Library · Services · System**, plus a `?` for Help and the avatar menu for Account (password, Google, sign out — nothing else). An Operator signs in and sees Live and a help icon. An Editor sees Live, Library, Services. An Administrator sees all four, and System holds four tabs: Status (what Health is now, minus the raw IDs), People (invites, roles, paired devices — moved out of Account), Setup, Log.

Vocabulary reduces to nine words: **Graphic** (absorbs overlay, cue, bug, scan card), **Slide** (absorbs panel-as-tab, multipart, numbered set), **Layout** (Lower third / Left panel / Right panel are its values), **Passage**, **Source** (absorbs book, edition, snapshot, baseline), **Service** (absorbs prepared service, collection, set, coverage, starter), **Draft**, **Published**, **Version**. Retire "optional" — it appears four times on one page and reads as an apology.

Visually, one rule does most of the work: buttons are rectangles, pills are only for state (PUBLISHED, DISCONNECTED, counts), nav is text with an underline. Today nav, status, counts, roles, layout names, filters and actions all render as the same rounded chip, so nothing tells you which ones do something. One level of card nesting; the page scrolls as one, only the on-air/preview panel is sticky. The colour semantics already in place are right and should be enforced: green goes to air (Show, Publish), red removes (Clear), amber needs attention, lavender is the live title; Show must be the only green thing in a row.

The ideal operator page, as a paragraph: a thin bar with the wordmark, a status dot and an avatar; a search field that has focus on load; today's service order on top of the full library below it, one row each with a thumbnail, the title, and the opening words; arrow keys move, Enter or the row's Show puts it on air, the live row stays lit; a sticky panel shows the artwork on air with Animate out and Clear under it. That is the whole app for Michael, and it fits above the fold on a booth laptop.

## Where the four audits disagreed, and what this report picks

How many nav items: 3, 4 or 5. This report picks four with role filtering, because an Operator seeing one item is the real win and the exact admin count barely matters.

Clear now: one audit proposed merging Animate out and Clear into a single "Out" that hard-clears on long-press. Rejected here — a hidden gesture under time pressure is a mode, and two clearly labelled buttons under the on-air panel are faster and safer.

Publish: one audit proposed a confirm sheet. Rejected — the exact saved render should simply be what the preview shows, and Publish should be one click.

Health: cut entirely versus fold into an admin System page. This report folds rather than cuts, since the provider-budget view is Daniel's own; but the readouts an operator could ever need are exactly one dot.

## What was not verified

The audits worked from captures of the live UI, not code. Two claims were dropped as unsupported: that invitation links are valid for only eight hours (the David Lazaroff row shows "expires in 8 h", which reads as a countdown; Help says 24 hours), and that the editor's siddur search requires a button press rather than filtering as you type (a Search button exists, but the behaviour was not exercised). Nothing was published, shown, or changed in production during the review.
