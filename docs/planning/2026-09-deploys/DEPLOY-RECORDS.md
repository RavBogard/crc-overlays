## Deploy record 20 — the cue log

**2026-09-14, 16:4x CT web; 16:21 CT relay.** Relay `c8ce9c519a579c58aca33c713ff867a3789d9a8b`, web `85d6b4ee67f229b62c7f3144584794e9b962218f`. The order of record is `docs/planning/2026-09-14-integration/HANDOFF-CODE-CUE-LOG-2026-09-14.md` with the plan beside it (ruling 7 of the cross-app integration sitting).

**The gap it closes.** Every accepted command was sequenced, durably committed, acknowledged — and then forgotten. Receipts key on `(action, cue)` for de-duplication only. There was no machine-readable record of what a service actually did, which is what centralreform.live needs to reconcile an “as performed” version beside the plan it published.

**What is kept.** One row per **accepted** command, appended in the Durable Object after the commit and before the broadcast: `{seq, at, action, cueId, source, serviceRef, sourceIds}`. `action` is `in`, `out`, `clear`, `cut`, `bug` or `history_cleared`; `source` is `control`, `companion` or `mcp` — **where a command came from, never who sent it**; `serviceRef` is a prepared-service collection id when one is known (a names panel names its own, and a caller may state one); `sourceIds` are the `library:` pins of the graphic that was on screen. The append is a side effect outside the command's transaction, in its own try/catch: **a history that cannot be written never refuses, delays or rolls back a graphic.** A command the relay does not accept — a replayed id, a stale controller sequence — leaves no row.

**The bound.** 2,000 rows or 14 days, whichever is smaller, enforced on every append and every read. Older rows are **dropped, not archived**. The administrator's clear-history deletes every row and logs the clearing itself as the only row left.

**What leaves the building.** `GET /api/history?since=&until=&after=&limit=` — at most 500 rows and 256 KiB a page, paging by `seq` — joins `sourceIds` to the siddur library into `{unitId, momentId, book, folio}` and **drops the ids**. Each row is rebuilt key by key and then walked in production, not only in tests, so a relay that one day answered with more than it should still could not publish it. The join happens on the way out rather than at publication, which is why a moments table adopted later will answer for services already recorded, with nothing republished. No CORS header: no browser on another site reads it. Readable by an Administrator or Editor, or by a **`history_reader`** credential — a third device kind that satisfies that one permission and nothing else (not read, not live control, not authoring), the mirror of the `setlist_reader` this workspace holds on centralreform.live. Neither shared key reaches it. An Administrator mints one in System → People → Paired devices and copies the token once. `get_service_history` gives an MCP client the same answer under the same bound.
